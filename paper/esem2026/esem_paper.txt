1 Introduction
Autonomous agents built on large language models (LLMs) increasingly execute production
workflows in customer support, code review, and incident response [5, 7, 6]. Unlike traditional
software, agents operate through stochastic loops ofplanning,tool execution,reasoning, and
delegation. As they enter production, observability has become critical, and a growing
empirical body shows that multi-agent failures are dominated by agent-specific pathologies—
specification, inter-agent misalignment, and task verification—rather than the network errors
that dominate microservice incidents [4].
The de-facto industry standard for distributed tracing, OpenTelemetry (OTel), defines a
metamodel for spans whoseSpanKind enumeration (CLIENT, SERVER, INTERNAL, PRODUCER,
CONSUMER) was designed for microservices. Two AI-specific extensions have emerged:
OpenTelemetry GenAI [11] introduces typed operation names including create_agent,
invoke_agent, execute_tool, and retrieval; OpenInference [1] introduces ten typed span
kinds including AGENT, GUARDRAIL, RETRIEVER, and TOOL. Both narrow the gap relative
to vanilla OTel. Neither, however, exposes typed sub-operations for the cognitive and
coordination phases that distinguish agent workflows: planning step generation, reasoning
chains, peer-agent delegation with typed source/target identifiers, memory operations, or
constrained guardrail outcomes.
This paper poses an empirical question that has not been answered in prior work:how
much of the observed cross-stack agent-fault detection variance is attributable to metamodel
choice versus per-framework instrumentation defaults?We construct a controlled fault-
injection harness, adopt the multi-agent failure taxonomy (MAST) of an independent prior
team [4] as externally-anchored ground truth, and survey the default tracing emissions of six
AI-agent framework SDKs as of 2026-05 with pinned source-survey evidence for each SDK.
We then measure Typed Capability Rate (TCR) and False-Positive Rate (FPR) under three
scoring rules: standardized-field dispatch, permissive substring matching on surveyed default
span names, and extended-attribute dispatch restricted to attributesactually publishedin
each metamodel’s specification.
What this paper doesnotclaim.
The DSM under study was designed by us to add typed kinds for the five phases the industry
baselines omit, so the capability-envelope gap is a quantification of a designed gap on a
uniform measurement instrument, not an empirical surprise. Our contribution is therealized
measurement: per-framework coverage grounded in a direct survey of each SDK’s source,
scoring restricted to attributes actually published in each metamodel’s convention, and

2 Background
2.1 The OpenTelemetry Span Metamodel
OpenTelemetry defines a span as the basic unit of execution in a distributed trace, carrying
a name, typed SpanKind, parent reference, timestamps, status, attributes, events, and
a Baggage map propagated by W3C Trace Context [13]. The five canonical SpanKind
literals (CLIENT, SERVER, INTERNAL, PRODUCER, CONSUMER) were designed for microservice
request–response interactions.
2.2 The Two AI-Specific Conventions
OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions.
[11] adds agen_ai.operation.name attribute with values includingchat, create_agent,
invoke_agent, execute_tool, retrieval, embeddings; plus a small attribute namespace
(gen_ai.usage.{input,output}_tokens,gen_ai.tool.name,gen_ai.agent.{id,name}).
OpenInference.
[1] defines openinference.span.kind with ten typed values: LLM, EMBEDDING, CHAIN,
RETRIEVER, RERANKER, TOOL, AGENT, GUARDRAIL, EVALUATOR, PROMPT; plus free-form attrib-
utes (tool.name,retrieval.documents, etc.).
2.3 The Coverage Gap and the MAST Taxonomy
Both AI-specific conventions provide typed entities for thevisiblesurface of an agent (LLM
call, tool execution, retrieval, agent lifecycle) but neither exposes typed sub-operations for
thecognitive(planning, reasoning),coordination(peer-agent delegation with typed identifiers,
memory), orpolicy(constrained guardrail outcomes) surfaces. An independent prior team
[4] presents the Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy (MAST), classifying failures into 14
modes across system design (FM-1.1–FM-1.5), inter-agent misalignment (FM-2.1–FM-2.6),
and task verification (FM-3.1–FM-3.3). We adopt MAST as the externally defined reference
taxonomy against which our fault-injection benchmark is mapped (Section 3.3).
3 Method
3.1 Research Questions
RQ1 How does the typed-capability footprint (TCR/FPR) of the three industry telemetry
metamodels compare on a controlled benchmark of 14 agent-specific fault classes, and
is any observed gap between metamodels statistically significant under a paired binary
analysis with multiple-comparison correction and effect-size estimation?
RQ2 To what extent does a DSM that adds typed sub-operations for delegation, planning,
reasoning, guardrails, and memory expand the typed-capability envelope relative to
the industry baselines, and how does the gap survive the obvious counter-experiments—
specifically (a) a permissive substring-matching scoring rule that lifts the baselines and
(b) per-adapter implementation variance characterized by Wilson CIs and the exact
small-cluster adapter range?

Terminology.
We useTyped Capability Rate (TCR)rather than “Fault Detection Rate (FDR)” to avoid
the collision with theFalse Discovery Rateof multiple-testing literature. TCR is defined as
the fraction of injected faults for which the standardized-field dispatch (or, where indicated,
permissive) predicate fires on the exported trace. TCR is a binary detection metricper fault
class, not a per-instance recall measure; Section 6 discusses the construct-validity scope of
this definition.
3.2 The Domain-Specific Metamodel (DSM)
We define the DSM as a metamodel conforming to OMG MOF [10], expressed in Ecore
[12]. Figure 1 gives the abstract syntax. The root container is Trace, which owns a
collection of AgentSpan instances. AgentSpan is abstract; nine concrete subclasses cover the
agent operations one-to-one with aSpanKind enumeration: AGENT, LLM_CALL, TOOL_CALL,
PLANNING, REASONING, DELEGATION, GUARD_RAIL, RETRIEVAL, MEMORY. Five of the nine kinds
(PLANNING, REASONING, DELEGATION, GUARD_RAIL, MEMORY) are absent from both AI-specific
industry conventions.
The DSM is accompanied by six OCL well-formedness invariants [9]; Listing 1 shows
two representative ones; the full set is released as an executableagentmm.ocl file in the
replication package.
3.3 Fault Taxonomy and MAST Mapping
We constructed a benchmark of 14 agent-specific fault types and mapped each onto a mode

fourteen faults map directly onto MAST modes;cost_explosion is our addition, motivated
by published LLM-agent cost-runaway postmortems and not present in MAST (which
predates large-scale agent deployment).

3.5 Detection Rule (Scoring) and Counter-Experiment
For each (telemetry condition, fault) pair we hand-coded a deterministic Python predicate over
the exported span list (one branch per fault, four banks for vanilla / GenAI / OpenInference
/ DSM). We report results underthreescoring rules, side-by-side, applied uniformly across
all four metamodels:
(a) Standardized-field dispatch (primary rule).Detectors may use only fields stand-
ardized by the metamodel under test: typed enum fields (gen_ai.operation.name,
openinference.span.kind, or agentmm.span.kind) plus structured standard fields
such as status, duration, tool name, token usage, retrieval metadata, or cost. Free-
form span names and substring heuristics are not allowed. This is the apples-to-apples
comparison of standardized semantics across metamodels.
(b) Permissive substring-matching (counter-experiment rule).Detectors may ad-
ditionally fire on substring matches againstspan.name (e.g. matching plan, reason,
deleg, guardrail, or memory). This rule lifts the baselines on faults whose phase-name
appears in the framework’s span naming convention but, as we report (Section 5),
introduces naming-collision FPR. We pre-specify the regex per fault in the artifact’s
permissive_predicates.py and apply it uniformly to every metamodel including the
DSM.
(c) Extended-attribute dispatch (deployed-baseline counterfactual).Detectors may
dispatch on any standardized free-form attribute that the metamodel under test specifies
in its conventions (e.g.gen_ai.tool.name, retrieval.documents, tool.parameters),
in addition to typed kinds. This is the most realistic “extended-baseline” counterfactual—
what a sufficiently disciplined practitioner could implement on top of OpenInference or
OTel GenAI without leaving the published convention. We pre-specify the per-metamodel
attribute set in the artifact’sextended_predicates.py.
We adopt rule (a) as the primary scoring rule (apples-to-apples typed semantics across
metamodels) and report (b) and (c) in parallel to forestall the obvious counter-experiments.

3.6 Design Freeze and Threats Prevention
Design sequence frozen workload→MAST mapping→DSM mapping→detector code (see
workload_design.md relative timestamps and blinded digests). The benchmark forestalls six
threats: (i) deterministic seed plus real-LLM sanity check (Section 4.3); (ii) uniform predicate
code with all three rules in parallel; (iii) mocked LLMs prevent provider quirks; (iv) the
reference adapter is a harness sanity check, with all discussion-level claims based on the
third-party mean and the exact across-adapter range; (v) MAST mapping is independently
re-coded (κ+ bootstrap CI); (vi) workload timestamped 32 days prior to the first taxonomy
commit, breaking workload→taxonomy→DSM circularity.

Privacy-Level Sensitivity
The DSM defines a three-level privacy filter (NONE, METADATA_ONLY, FULL); the default
(METADATA_ONLY) provides full diagnostic signal without exposing prompt or completion
content. All DSM detectors operate on typed structure and metadata attributes only, so
payload redaction is TCR-neutral by DSM design — bothMETADATA_ONLY and FULL achieve
identical aggregate TCR (0.429on third-party adapters under survey-grounded coverage;
1.000on the reference). The TCR-neutral redaction property is a DSM-specific design claim
that the three industry baselines do not specify in their conventions.

5 Discussion
The conformance gap is a framework gap, not a metamodel gap.
The 4.5×realized cross-adapter range (0.143to0 .643) is dominated by per-framework
instrumentation defaults, not by metamodel choice. anthropic_sdk sits at the bottom
because it has no built-in tracing module (verified via full-text search ofsrc/anthropic/
for opentelemetry|tracer|telemetry: zero matches outside the Managed Agents server-
response models); openai_sdk = openai-agents-python sits at the top because it already
publishes typed handoff (with from_agent/to_agent, the cleanest typed-DELEGATION
primitive of any framework surveyed) and typedguardrail(with explicittriggered: bool ,
the cleanest typed-GUARDRAIL primitive). The actionable open work isper-framework
instrumentation completeness, not metamodel extension or DSM design.
Deployable recommendation.
For an SRE today the empirical recommendation isframework-dependent, not metamodel-
dependent. (i)Ifthedeploymentrunson openai-agents-python, theexistingtyped handoff
and guardrail spans already deliver9/14 = 0.643realized DSM-equivalent coverage at FPR
0.000without any metamodel migration. (ii) If the deployment uses the bare Anthropic
SDK, no metamodel choice rescues coverage above2/14 = 0.143until the SDK adds typed
agent/tool/handoff/guardrail spans (or a richer external instrumentation package is adopted).
The0 .571gap that prior work has framed as a metamodel-design gap is, on inspection, a
per-framework instrumentation gap.
Construct caveat.
TCRisbinaryperfaultandistherightper-faultexpressivityproxyforacontrolledbenchmark;
operational metrics (mean-time-to-detect, attribution accuracy, alert precision-at-action) are
out of scope and would matter for adoption decisions in production.
6 Threats to Validity
Construct validity.
TCR is binary per fault class on a rate-1 mocked-LLM setup; it measuresmetamodel-with-
framework expressivity, not production recall. The realized cross-adapter ranking is scoped
to this benchmark, the surveyed framework versions as of 2026-05, and the conformant
scoring rules; with stochastic LLMs, realistic trace volumes, or framework-version drift the
TCR/FPR ranking can change. Some predicates use benchmark-provided oracle fields (for
example intended tool, configured cost budget, injected memory corruption, or evaluator ver-
dict); telemetry_field_audit.tsv classifies each predicate input as production-plausible or

benchmark-oracle; Section 4.2 reports the corresponding production-plausible-only sensitivity.
Detector banks for OpenInference and OTel GenAI are restricted to attributesactually
publishedin each metamodel’s specification (Section 3.5), eliminating the constructive-bias
threat that an earlier pilot of this work suffered from. No independent OpenInference or OTel
GenAI practitioner audit of the conformant detectors was conducted; replication packages
and attribute-key citations are released. The releaseddetector_fairness_checklist.tsv
is therefore an author-judgment audit, not an external endorsement.
Internal validity (constructive bias).
Our 14-fault benchmark and the nine DSM kinds were authored by us; we use the
externally-authored MAST taxonomy [4] as ground-truth labels for the 13 mapped faults
(cost_explosion is independently motivated). The omitted MAST modes (FM-1.2, FM-2.2)
are exactly where typedAGENT.role and GUARD_RAIL kinds are plausible, so they bound the
claim. Per-framework DSM-kind coverage is grounded in a direct survey of each SDK’s source
as of 2026-05 with pinned commit SHAs and file/line citations incoverage_matrix.md
and source_survey_evidence.tsv, eliminating the assertion-based per-profile claims of
prior versions. Mitigations are the second-annotator MAST mapping (κ= 0.83), freeze
log, third-party headline, per-kind ablation, and permissive/extended counter-experiments
restricted to published attributes.
External validity.
The adapter profiles are executable simulations of telemetry capabilities grounded in source-
survey evidence, not live package integrations. The mocked-LLM setup measures detector-
existence, not production recall. The live-CLI real-LLM sanity check (Section 4.3) verifies
harness behavior but does not generalize. We claim no generalization beyond the surveyed
framework versions, the 14-fault MAST-anchored taxonomy, mocked LLMs, and the three
scoring rules.
Conclusion validity.
Per-cell TCR is deterministic so point estimates are exact; we report Wilson 95% CIs,
descriptive McNemar/Holm-3/Newcombe/Cohen’s-h for the capability-envelope designed
gap, exact adapter range[0.143, 0.643], and triangulation showing the absence of a statistically
detectable cross-adapter DSM advantage under survey-grounded coverage (GEE-on-adapter
p = 0.264, GEE-on-fault p = 0.525; sign-flip two-sidedp = 0.75). The n = 14design
resolves only very large effects (∆min = 0.50); the realized cross-adapter contrast at the
surveyed-framework scale is descriptive only.
7 Conclusion
Within this benchmark and the surveyed SDKs, framework instrumentation defaults dominate
deployable fault-detection coverage: realized DSM spans4.5×, from2 /14on Anthropic
SDK to9/14on OpenAI Agents. Under published-attribute scoring, default DSM reaches
(36/84, 4/72)versus OpenInference extended(34 /84, 0/36); threshold tuning moves DSM
to(36 /84, 0/72). The cross-adapter difference is statistically inconclusive (GEEp = 0.264,
sign-flipp = 0.75), so the practical open work is per-framework instrumentation completeness.
